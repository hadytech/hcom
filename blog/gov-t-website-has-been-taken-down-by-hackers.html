Gov't website has been taken down by hackers

DATE: 2026.08.27 / CATEGORY: ADVISORY / READ TIME: 3 min / AUTHOR: OSEC RESEARCH TEAM

Cyber Incident Alert: Niger Government Commerce Subdomain Defaced

In the ever-evolving landscape of global cybersecurity, government websites remain prime targets for digital vandals, hacktivists, and cybercriminals. Recently, a specific subdomain belonging to the government of Niger appears to have fallen victim to a defacement attack. The targeted URL, https://infosniger.commerce.gouv.ne/pwnd.html, clearly indicates a breach within the infrastructure of Niger's Ministry of Commerce (commerce.gouv.ne).

The Defacement

The extent of the visual defacement is captured in the provided image (thumbnail). The perpetrators replaced the standard webpage content with a classic, stark hacker aesthetic: a pitch-black background dominated by glowing, terminal-green text.

The message left behind is a common trope in website defacements, aiming to mock the site's defenses. The page bluntly states: "You have been hacked!"

"Security is just an illusion"

The attackers also made sure to leave their calling cards, attributing the breach to individuals or groups going by the monikers: Zero X Zero Team_CC Gh0st@Terminal

Understanding the Attack

Website defacements like this one are the digital equivalent of spray-painting graffiti on a building. While they are highly visible and embarrassing for the victim organization, they often represent a relatively low-level compromise.

Usually, these attacks exploit known vulnerabilities in content management systems (CMS), insecure plugins, or weak administrative credentials. The attackers gain enough access to upload a new HTML file (in this case, pwnd.html) or alter the index page, but they may not necessarily have breached deeper, more sensitive databases.

However, the ominous message, "Security is just an illusion," serves as a stark reminder. Even if this specific incident is just superficial digital vandalism, a successful defacement proves that unauthorized access was achieved. It raises immediate questions about the overall security posture of the gouv.ne infrastructure.

The Broader Implication

For government entities, maintaining a secure online presence is critical for public trust and operational integrity. An incident like this highlights the necessity for continuous vulnerability scanning, timely software patching, and robust access controls.

While the "Ghost at the Terminal" has made their point for now, incidents like the one at infosniger.commerce.gouv.ne underscore that in the digital realm, security must be an active, ongoing process, not just an illusion.
